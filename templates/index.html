How it works

Five signals, five different questions

Every answer traces back to official federal aviation records - monthly passenger, seat and departure counts for every US airport since 2014. Each signal reads them a different way.

01

Congestion how full is it now?

Share of available seats actually sold, averaged over the last six months, plus the peak month.

02

Growth which direction?

Yearly passenger growth, recovery against 2019, whether momentum is speeding up or slowing, and whether airlines are adding seats fast enough to keep pace.

03

Candidate score which one do we pick?

Congestion and growth combined into one ranking, so several airports can be compared side by side.

04

Traffic mix what should we build?

International share and average trip length - the two things that decide whether you need customs halls and wide-body gates, or domestic gates and faster security.

05

National rank how big, gaining or losing?

Position among all 1,311 US airports, and how far it has climbed or fallen in ten years. An airport can grow every year and still lose ground.

Every number, same date

Growth and rank are measured over the same period, so they are directly comparable - no stale figure quietly sitting beside a fresh one.

Whether vs what

Four signals count how many passengers. Traffic mix asks who they are - two airports can look identical yet need completely different buildings.

What it won't answer

No delays, gate counts, fares or route-level detail. That data isn't in this table, and the agent says so rather than guessing.
